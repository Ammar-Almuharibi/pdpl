Personal Data Protection Law

A compliance professional's visual study guide to Saudi Arabia's PDPL — all 43 articles distilled into structured reference material.

Effective 2023 43 Articles SDAIA Supervised Kingdom of Saudi Arabia

Law at a Glance

Art. 1–3, 42–43
Scope: Applies to any processing of personal data of individuals in Saudi Arabia — including processing done by entities outside the Kingdom if it targets Saudi residents. Deceased persons' data is covered if it could identify them or their family.
Art. 1

Personal Data

Any data — regardless of form or source — that leads to identifying an individual specifically or indirectly. Includes names, ID numbers, photos, videos, bank details, and more.

Art. 1

Processing

Any operation on personal data: collecting, recording, storing, organizing, modifying, retrieving, using, disclosing, transmitting, publishing, sharing, blocking, erasing, or destroying.

Art. 1 (18–19)

Controller vs Processor

The Controller decides the purpose and manner of processing. The Processor processes data on behalf of the Controller. The Controller remains responsible to the data subject.

Art. 2

Exclusions

Personal and family use is excluded — provided the data subject has not published or disclosed the data to others. The Regulations define what counts as personal/family use.

Art. 30

Competent Authority

SDAIA (Saudi Data & AI Authority) oversees implementation. The Saudi Central Bank retains oversight of financial data within its jurisdiction.

Art. 42–43

Key Dates

Issued September 2021. Amended March 2023. Came into force 720 days after publication. Implementing Regulations issued within 720 days of the original law.

The compliance lifecycle

1

Map your data

Identify all personal data your organisation collects, its source, purpose, retention period, and any third parties it is shared with. This feeds your Art. 31 processing records.

2

Establish lawful bases

For each processing activity, identify the lawful basis (Art. 5–6). Consent is the default; document exceptions clearly. Sensitive data has stricter rules — no legitimate interest basis.

3

Draft your privacy policy

Art. 12 requires a privacy policy made available before collection. It must cover the purpose, data types, means, storage, destruction, and data subject rights.

4

Implement technical & organisational safeguards

Art. 19 requires all necessary organisational, administrative and technical measures to protect data — including during transfer. Art. 22 mandates impact assessments.

5

Build breach response procedures

Art. 20: notify SDAIA upon knowing of a breach. Notify data subjects if the breach could damage their data or prejudice their rights. Timelines are set by the Regulations.

6

Operationalise data subject rights

Art. 21: respond to data subject requests within Regulations timelines. Build workflows for access, correction, portability, erasure, and consent withdrawal.

Data Subject Rights

Art. 4–5, 7, 9, 21
Art. 4(1) · Art. 13

Right to be Informed

Data subjects must be told: the legal basis and purpose of collection, which data is mandatory vs optional, who it will be shared with, cross-border transfer status, consequences of not providing data, and their rights.

Art. 4(2) · Art. 9

Right of Access

Data subjects may access their personal data held by the Controller. Controllers may set time frames (per Regulations) and may restrict access for data subject/third-party protection or public entity security needs.

Art. 4(3)

Right to Portability

Data subjects may request their personal data in a readable and clear format. Controls and procedures are set by the Regulations.

Art. 4(4) · Art. 17

Right to Correct

Data subjects may request correction, completion, or update of their data. Upon correction, the Controller must notify all other entities to which the data was transferred.

Art. 4(5) · Art. 11(4) · Art. 18

Right to Erasure

Data subjects may request destruction of data no longer needed. However, the Controller may retain data if required by law, judicial proceedings, or if it is anonymised (no longer identifying).

Art. 5(2)

Right to Withdraw Consent

Consent may be withdrawn at any time. Controllers cannot use consent as a condition of service unless processing is directly necessary for that service (Art. 7).

Lawful Bases for Processing

Art. 5–6, 10, 25–27
Default rule (Art. 5): Personal data may not be processed, and the purpose of processing may not be changed, without the consent of the data subject — unless one of the Article 6 exceptions applies.

Exceptions to consent (Art. 6)

Art. 6(1)

Vital Interest

Processing serves the actual interests of the data subject, but communicating with them is impossible or difficult.

Art. 6(2)

Legal / Contractual Obligation

Processing is required by another law, or to implement a prior agreement to which the data subject is a party.

Art. 6(3)

Public Entity — Security / Judicial

The Controller is a Public Entity and processing is required for security purposes or to satisfy judicial requirements.

Art. 6(4)

Legitimate Interest

Necessary for the Controller's legitimate interest, without prejudice to the data subject's rights. Cannot be used for sensitive data. Controls set by Regulations.

Not for sensitive data

Collection from third-party sources (Art. 10)

By default, Controllers may only collect data directly from the data subject. Collection from other sources is allowed when: consent is given; data is publicly available; the Controller is a Public Entity acting for public interest/security/judicial purposes; compliance would harm the data subject; necessary to protect public health or individual life/health; data will be anonymised; or necessary for legitimate interest (not sensitive data).

Special processing rules

Art. 25

Direct Marketing Communications

Controllers cannot use personal contact details (post, email, etc.) for advertising unless the recipient has given prior consent and a clear opt-out mechanism is provided. Exception: awareness materials from Public Entities.

Art. 26

Marketing of Personal Data

Personal data may be processed for marketing purposes only if: collected directly from the data subject, consent is given, and it is not sensitive data.

Sensitive data excluded
Art. 27

Research & Statistical Purposes

Processing without consent is allowed if the data does not specifically identify the subject; or identity will be destroyed before disclosure (if not sensitive data); or required by law or prior agreement.

Controller Obligations

Art. 8, 11–14, 17–22, 25, 30–31, 41
A

Privacy Policy — Art. 12

Must publish a privacy policy available to data subjects before collection. Must specify: purpose, data types collected, collection means, processing/storage/destruction methods, and data subject rights and how to exercise them.

B

Data Minimisation — Art. 11

Collection purpose must directly relate to the Controller's activities and not contravene law. Collection methods must be direct, clear, secure, and free of deception. Data content must be limited to the minimum necessary. Once data is no longer needed, the Controller must cease collection and destroy it without undue delay.

C

Accuracy — Art. 14

Controllers may not process data without taking sufficient steps to verify accuracy, completeness, timeliness, and relevance to the collection purpose.

D

Security Measures — Art. 19

Must implement all necessary organisational, administrative and technical measures to protect data — including during transfer. Specific controls in the Regulations.

E

Breach Notification — Art. 20

Must notify SDAIA upon knowing of any breach, damage, or illegal access. Must also notify data subjects if the breach could damage their data or prejudice their rights and interests. Timelines and procedures per Regulations.

F

Processing Records — Art. 31

Must maintain records (for a Regulations-defined period) including: Controller contact details, processing purpose, categories of data subjects, entities to which data is/will be disclosed, whether data is/will be transferred outside the Kingdom, and expected retention period. Records must be available to SDAIA on request.

G

Impact Assessments — Art. 22

Must conduct a personal data processing impact assessment for any product or service, based on the nature of the Controller's activity. Details in Regulations.

H

Processor Oversight — Art. 8

May only use Processors that provide necessary guarantees of PDPL compliance. Must monitor Processor compliance. This does not absolve the Controller of responsibility to data subjects or SDAIA.

I

Perpetual Confidentiality — Art. 41

Any person processing personal data must protect its confidentiality even after the end of their occupational or contractual relationship with the Controller.

Retention & destruction (Art. 18)

Must destroy when…
Must retain when…
Data is no longer needed for the purpose for which it was collected
A legal basis requires retention for a specific period (destroy on expiry or purpose satisfaction, whichever is longer)
Purpose of collection has ceased and no legal basis for retention exists
Data is related to a case before a judicial authority (destroy once proceedings conclude)

Sensitive & Special Data

Art. 1(11–15), 23–28
Higher bar: Sensitive data cannot use the "legitimate interest" lawful basis. Disclosure or publishing of sensitive data with intent to harm or for personal benefit is a criminal offence.

What counts as sensitive data?

Racial / ethnic origin Religious belief Political / intellectual belief Security / criminal convictions Biometric data (for identification) Genetic data Health data Unknown parentage

Health data rules (Art. 23)

Minimum Access

Access to health data (including medical files) must be restricted to the minimum number of employees necessary to provide the required health services.

Minimum Processing

Health data processing operations must be restricted to the minimum extent possible as necessary to provide health services or offer health insurance programs.

Credit data rules (Art. 24)

Explicit Consent Required

Appropriate measures must verify the data subject's explicit consent for collection, change of purpose, or disclosure/publishing of credit data — per both the PDPL and the Credit Information Law.

Notification of Disclosure Requests

The data subject must be notified whenever a disclosure request for their credit data is received from any entity.

Official documents (Art. 28)

Copying official documents where data subjects are identifiable is not permitted unless required by law or a competent public authority requests it per the Regulations.

Cross-Border Transfers

Art. 29
Permitted purposes: Cross-border transfer or disclosure to a party outside the Kingdom is allowed to perform an obligation under an international agreement to which the Kingdom is a party; to serve the interests of the Kingdom; to perform an obligation to which the data subject is a party; or for other purposes defined in the Regulations.

Conditions that must always be met (Art. 29(2))

No Harm to National Security

The transfer or disclosure must not cause any prejudice to national security or the vital interests of the Kingdom.

Adequate Protection Level

The receiving country or entity must provide a level of protection at least equivalent to that guaranteed by the PDPL and Regulations — assessed by SDAIA in coordination with relevant authorities.

Data Minimisation

The transfer or disclosure must be limited to the minimum amount of personal data needed for the stated purpose.

Emergency exception (Art. 29(3)): The adequacy and minimisation conditions do not apply in cases of extreme necessity to preserve the life or vital interests of the data subject, or to prevent, examine, or treat disease.

Key points for compliance teams

You may transfer when…
You may NOT transfer when…
The destination country has been assessed as providing adequate protection
The transfer could harm national security or vital Kingdom interests
Transfer is necessary to fulfil a contract the data subject is party to
The receiving entity does not meet the PDPL-equivalent protection standard
Transfer serves Kingdom interests under an international agreement
More data than strictly necessary is being transferred
Life/health emergency (adequacy condition waived)
The transfer lacks any of the permitted purposes in Art. 29(1)

Penalties & Enforcement

Art. 34–40
Criminal · Art. 35
2 yrs + SAR 3M
Intentional disclosure or publishing of sensitive data in violation of the PDPL, with intent to harm the data subject or achieve personal benefit. Prosecution by the Public Prosecution before the competent court. Fine doubles on recidivism.
Administrative · Art. 36
Warning or up to SAR 5M
Any violation of PDPL or Regulations not covered by Art. 35. Imposed by a SDAIA-appointed violations committee (minimum 3 members, including technical specialist and legal advisor). Fine doubles on repeat violation.
Civil Compensation · Art. 40
Proportionate damages
Any individual who suffers material or moral damage as a result of a PDPL or Regulations violation may apply to a competent court for compensation. Independent of the criminal or administrative penalty.
Disciplinary · Art. 39
Employer discipline
Public entities must discipline employees who violate the PDPL or Regulations in accordance with applicable disciplinary laws and procedures — in addition to other penalties.

Enforcement process

1

Complaint or detection

Data subjects may submit complaints directly to SDAIA (Art. 34). SDAIA-appointed inspectors may also detect violations directly (Art. 37), with power to seek assistance from criminal investigation authorities.

2

Violations committee reviews (Art. 36)

A committee of at least 3 members (including a technical specialist and legal advisor) examines the violation, considering its type, seriousness, and impact. Issues a warning or fine. Decision must be approved by the SDAIA president.

3

Right of appeal (Art. 36(3))

The violator may appeal the committee's decision before the competent court.

4

Additional orders (Art. 38)

Courts may order confiscation of funds obtained through violations. Courts or the committee may also order publication of a summary of the judgment at the violator's expense — after the judgment becomes final.

Seizure power (Art. 37(3)): SDAIA has the right to seize the means or tools used in committing a violation until a decision is made on it.
